# Stockholm Weekend: Sat 17 to Sun 18 Oct 2026

Saturday is a full day. Sunday ends around 16:00.
Mid-October: about 6–10 °C, sunset around 17:25, and showers are likely. Bring a layer.

**Getting around:** tap a contactless card or your phone on SL readers (metro, trams, buses and the Djurgården ferry all count). A single fare covers 75 minutes. Most of the centre is walkable.

**Heads-up:**
- *Dagens lunch*, the cheap weekday lunch deal, doesn't exist on weekends. Cheap local food on weekends means market halls, street food and bakeries.
- Almost everywhere is card-only. Cash is often refused.
- Systembolaget, the state alcohol shop, closes around 15:00 on Saturday and is closed on Sunday. Bars and restaurants serve as normal.

**$ / $$ / $$$:** roughly under 150 SEK / 150–300 SEK / 300+ SEK per person for a main.

## Unique, non-touristy experiences

1. **Sauna and a lake dip at Hellasgården** (Nacka nature reserve, about 25 min by bus 401 from Slussen). This is what Stockholmers actually do on autumn weekends: wood-fired sauna, then a jump into the cold lake from the jetty, then repeat. About 100 SEK for 1.5 h. You must **book online** (hellasgarden.se), check the mixed-sauna times (some slots are single-sex), and bring a towel and swimwear. The lake trail around Källtorpssjön is beautiful in autumn. It fits Saturday afternoon instead of Fotografiska.
2. **Centralbadet** (Drottninggatan 88): a 1904 art-nouveau bathhouse in the city centre with a pool, saunas and a garden courtyard. It's the rainy-day version of the sauna.
3. **Hötorget Sunday loppis:** browse Swedish glass, ceramics and vinyl with locals. Combine with Sunday morning.
4. **Sunset with a drink on Skinnarviksberget:** a locals' sunset rock, with no railings and no crowds.
5. **Långholmen:** a former prison island with forest trails along the water. Walk over from Hornstull. Good for a morning walk with a coffee in hand.
6. **Metro art crawl:** Kungsträdgården, T-Centralen, Solna Centrum (red sky, green forest) and Stadion (rainbow). It costs one ticket and takes about an hour.
7. **Ritorno for fika:** a 1950s café full of regulars. It's a time capsule, not a tourist stop.
8. **A proper beer-hall dinner at Pelikan:** order a *snaps* with your herring starter. It's what Swedes do at a "fancy-casual" dinner.
9. **Medieval Museum under Norrbro:** free, underground, with excavated city walls, and almost empty.
10. **Millesgården** (Lidingö, 30 min by metro and tram): a sculptor's home and terraced sculpture garden overlooking the water. Mostly visited by locals. A good half-day swap for Djurgården.
11. **Jazz at Fasching** on Saturday night: Stockholm's main jazz club, with a local crowd.
12. **Tunnbrödsrulle from a hot-dog stand:** the late-night local snack.

---

> **Check on the day:** autumn hours shift. Confirm the herring cart, Hellasgården sauna slots, Hötorget loppis, Söderhallarna's Sunday hours and Rosendals Trädgård (a biodynamic garden café on Djurgården that may already be closed for the season) before going.
